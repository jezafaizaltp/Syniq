# Measuring the pilot

The question the pilot has to answer: **did students who used SYNiq do better than they would have without it?**

SYNiq answers it with marks the school already has, so students sit no extra tests.

## The method

1. **Pilot group:** the 50 Class 9 students using SYNiq.
2. **Comparison group:** other Class 9 students at Providence who don't use SYNiq, ideally a similar number and a similar spread of marks. They need no SYNiq account.
3. **Before:** the last school exam in each subject before students start SYNiq (for example, the first term exam).
4. **After:** a school exam after some weeks of use (for example, the second term or annual exam). Both groups sit the same exam.
5. SYNiq works out, for each subject, how much each group's average changed, in percentage points. **The difference between the two changes is what SYNiq may have added.** If the exam was simply easier, both groups go up and the difference stays near zero.

It also shows:

- **A likely range** for that difference. If the whole range is above zero, the gain is clear. If it includes zero, it can't yet be told apart from chance.
- **By starting marks:** the change for students who started in the lower, middle and upper third. This answers whether SYNiq helps students who were struggling as much as everyone else, which is why the pilot students were chosen across mark bands.
- **By practice:** whether pilot students who answered more questions in SYNiq gained more than those who answered fewer.

## How to do it

On the admin's **School** page, open **Pilot results**.

1. **Add a test:** name, subject, maximum marks, date, and whether it was before or after SYNiq started.
2. **Upload its marks** as a sheet with two columns, `student` and `marks`:
   - Pilot students: their SYNiq login from the login sheet.
   - Comparison students: a code, C1, C2, C3 and so on. Keep the list of which code is which student at school, on paper or in the school's own files, never in SYNiq. Use the same code for the same student every time.
   - Absent: write AB.
3. SYNiq checks the whole sheet first and saves nothing if any row is wrong: names instead of logins, unknown logins, marks above the maximum, or a pilot student whose parent has not consented.
4. Once a subject has a before and an after test, its results appear at the top of the page.
5. **Download anonymous results** gives one row per student and subject, without names, logins or codes, in random order, for a research report.
6. When the report is written, **Delete all results**.

## Privacy

- Marks are stored apart from everything else and only the school admin can see them. The teaching engine never reads them, so no student is treated differently because of their marks. Teachers, parents and students never see them in SYNiq.
- Results are only shown as group averages. Any group of fewer than 5 students is hidden.
- The starting-mark thirds are worked out for the report only and never stored or shown against a student.
- Comparison students are never named in SYNiq.
- Pilot students whose parents withdraw consent are left out of the results automatically.

**Consent form:** the paper consent form for pilot families should say that the school will share their child's term exam marks with SYNiq to measure, as group averages only, whether SYNiq helped, and that the marks are deleted after the pilot. Using comparison students' marks as anonymous codes is the school's own evaluation, but the school should confirm it is comfortable with this. A privacy lawyer should check both before the pilot.

## What it can and can't show

- With 50 students, only fairly large effects will show clearly. A range that includes zero means "not proven yet", not "it didn't work".
- The groups are not chosen at random, so other differences between them (different teachers, more motivated families) can affect results. A comparison group taught by the same teachers helps.
- Term exams cover more than what students practised in SYNiq, especially early on when only some chapters have content. Comparing subjects with lots of SYNiq content against subjects with little is a useful check.
